An app's 5 MiB of storage in a scope now counts each key's bytes eight times, beside the 128 bytes for the key and its value's bytes. The device keeps a key four times (the row's own key, the stored row and both indexes), three of them as UTF-16, about 10 bytes for each byte of the key: with the key counted once, a scope of 256-byte keys counted at 5 MiB took 40 to 55 MB of a browser's IndexedDB, and 64-byte keys 21 to 28 MB. Counted eight times, a scope holds about 2,400 keys of 256 bytes (13,600 before) and takes about 7 to 9 MB; short keys barely change (40 to 48 bytes more for a 5- or 6-byte key). Nothing changes in what is stored or in the key's 256-byte bound, so an older app keeps every key it has; a scope that is past 5 MiB by the new count takes no new key, and still takes a delete or a write that does not grow it. `MINI_APP_LIMITS.storageKeyTimes` carries the number for an app that plans its room.
