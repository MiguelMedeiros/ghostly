A reader takes an envelope until ten minutes past its `expires` by its own clock and refuses none for an `issued` ahead of it; `sequence`, which the reader keeps, is what stops an envelope from being taken twice, and `expires - issued` stays bounded. Before, an envelope dated more than 30 s past the reader's clock, or past its expiry, was dropped without a word: a contact two minutes ahead got no first contact, text or receipt through, and one five minutes behind had every text expire on arrival. The receipt for a text is owed for the lifetime the text had left, counted on the reader's clock. A sender dates `issued` up to two minutes before its clock (never before the envelope's lifetime began, nor more than 30 s before its text's time), so that apps up to 1.0.2, which keep the 30 s rule, take a control envelope from a clock up to two and a half minutes ahead and a text once it has been going out for as long as the clock is ahead. No wire change.
