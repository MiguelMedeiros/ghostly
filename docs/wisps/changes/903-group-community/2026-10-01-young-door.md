While no hub has been one for a minute (a group's first minute), the hub that has been one longest is at the door alone; between two of the same second, the lower key. Before, every hub listed lately counted. The first hub, alone in the beacon, reads it only when it republishes, so for up to half a minute it did not know that the first member it let in had become a hub; that member, counting both at the door, answered the next knock too when its key was the lower one. In real browsers on a test relay both answered in 4 runs of 6: the joiner got in through whichever side its answer reached (8.5 to 9.3 s), and the other hub kept an entry session looking fast for 90 s. On the headless engines, where two hubs on one entry session let nobody in, the third person waited 231 s in one run of 10 and was not in after six minutes in another; with one hub at the door, 5 to 9 s in all 10.
