A reader no longer refuses a pointer for its `issued`, nor a manifest or an item for a `ts` ahead of its clock, and allows an expiry up to one lifetime and a day past now: `rev` orders pointers and `seq` orders items, both kept by the reader. Before, a pointer issued more than a minute past the reader's clock was dropped without a word, and a manifest or an item dated that far ahead was refused, the item for good, with its sender told it "could not be verified as yours, or was too large", so nothing a contact whose clock ran two minutes fast held was ever read. A sender now dates its pointer and its manifest ten minutes before its clock, and ends the lifetimes it gives ten minutes early, so that apps up to 1.0.2 read a sender up to about eleven minutes ahead; they still refuse an item whose message time is more than a minute ahead of their clock when they fetch it. No wire change.
