The age of an offer is counted on the reader's clock: an offer is new when a read of the maker's record that the network answered did not have it and it is not dated before the maker's own packet that read found (both are the maker's clock), and it is then at most as old as the time since that read; an answer has no age to check, since it names the offer it answers, which the reader made in the attempt under way. Before, every signal whose `ts` was more than 120 seconds from the reader's clock, either way, was dropped without a word, so two devices whose clocks were two minutes apart never connected over WebRTC. A read that found nothing of the maker's counts only for a first contact (a first pairing before the pin, a group's link) and only for an offer dated within ten minutes of the reader's clock; an offer found at the first read of a run, or after a copy a transport kept, is still judged by its `ts`; a signal dated more than ten minutes ahead is not taken. An answer made again is timed from when its offer was taken, not from the offer's `ts`. Client policy, no wire change; an app up to 1.0.2 still drops the signals of a contact two minutes from it.
