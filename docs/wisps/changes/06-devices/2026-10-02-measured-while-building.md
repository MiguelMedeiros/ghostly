Revised with what building the first two parts of phase 1 measured and found, and with the review of the second. Relays ignore `If-Match` (a conditional put exists on the DHT only, and browsers reach the DHT only through relays), nothing guards an equal sequence (a DHT node replaces the packet even with a matching `cas`, a relay keeps the larger bytes), and a relay answers a read from its cache unless asked with `NetworkOnly`. So a put is no longer taken as proof that nobody else wrote: a device that raises the turn puts within 10 seconds of its read, waits `T` (30 seconds, resting on a visibility time that is still to be measured, with `T >= P + 2V` shown), reads every source again and goes active only if the record is still its own. The guarantee is restated: a normal handoff is safe with no timing, two devices raising the turn at once are kept apart by the wait while the network is in time and are otherwise detected at the next read, and every switch and takeover now ends with about half a minute in which no device is active. Also: only a packet whose signature verifies moves the highest sequence seen, never a number a relay reports; the result `closed` (a packet at or above the tombstone's sequence that is no tombstone) is no good read and has exits; a superseded or moving device that reads its own old packet stays; the packet is canonical (TTL 300, one TXT cut) and its measured size is 634 bytes; Desktop puts to the DHT first, on a Mainline node of the turn's own that is built at start and whose miss counts as no answer; limited mode refuses a message to a contact, as the offline engine does; the tombstone's fields, the slot count, the release kept through a turn, the tie-break at an equal `instance`, and a reader with nothing stored are specified. From the first part: no `DB_VERSION` number is pinned (1.0.2 has 10, dev has 12) and the first part that can freeze a copy raises it, with the files index as a version step of its own; the device record is keyed by the peer database's name, so an install writes it under both names; the stricter of the Desktop's two copies is defined by the engine-running state and then the write count; a state that cannot be read is closed only with evidence of a device set; the extension's gate sits in the offscreen document; the read-only open of a releasing device is defined; the undefined lineage field is reserved; and the lock's attempt counter is named as the one write that still lands in a frozen copy.
