`ghostly daemon restart` and `ghostly daemon --detach` now write their pid to `daemon.claim` in the profile's folder, touch it every second while they wait, and remove it once the daemon they start answers on its socket (or the start fails). While that claim is under 10 s old and its process alive, the profile's lock is refused (`busy`) to every process but the claimant and the daemon it spawns, and a one-shot command waits for the daemon's socket (up to 30 s per holder, as for a daemon starting) and goes through it. Before, the lock was free between the old daemon's stop and the new one's start, and one-shot commands that kept taking it in turn (a bot's typing indicator) left the new daemon never finding it free: the restart failed busy and the profile had no daemon.
