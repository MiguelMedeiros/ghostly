## What this profile is

> A group whose link is meant for a large community, open while the admin is away and for hundreds of members, is the [community profile](9xx-group-community.md). This one stays the small private group: contacts, or a link that works while the admin's app is open.

`group-mesh/1` lets up to 32 members exchange **text** over a full mesh of pairwise, authenticated data sessions, with a membership chain and epoch keys that exclude removed members from everything sent after their removal and admitted members from everything sent before their admission. It is the bounded mesh prototype 900 asked for, with three decisions made explicit:

1. **Topology: a mesh of dedicated pairwise edges, derived from the roster.** Every pair of members runs one [paired-chat/1](401-paired-chat.md) link of its own, whose rendezvous identities and discovery key both sides derive from the X25519 secret of their member keys and the group id (`edgeParams` in [`groupCrypto.ts`](../../packages/core/src/groupCrypto.ts)). Nobody distributes edge parameters, nobody but the two members can compute them, and the edge's paired session is pinned in advance to the member keys the roster names, so admission of a member is the only trust step. Edges go over WebRTC, and over Iroh or HyperDHT with a member whose app has no WebRTC (§ Transports). Past 16 members, while members whose apps stay online are there, the others keep edges with two of them only, and they pass everything on (§ Hubs).
2. **Delivery: the author sends to every member directly, and any member hands on what another missed.** A message goes over each open edge. A member whose edge to the author is down gets it later from the author's bounded log (32 messages, 128 KiB), or from any other member that received it and is asked for it (§ Catch-up). Every message is signed by its author, so a member handing it on can withhold it but not forge or alter it, and per-sender order and gap detection stay honest ([400](400-chat.md)). There is no server store: a member nobody who holds the message meets again does not get it.
3. **Coordinator: exactly one admin per epoch.** The admin signs every membership commit; there is no election. The role is transferred by a commit; an admin who loses its key leaves a group that can only be re-formed, as 900 says.

Payments between two members are part of it (§ Payments): the money travels on their edge, the group sees a note. Files, media, calls and local services are not part of this profile and are refused in groups.

## Keys and epochs

| Object | Lifetime | Who knows it |
|---|---|---|
| Member key (Ed25519) | The member's participation in one group; never reused across groups | Public in the roster; seed only on the member's device |
| Epoch secret (32 random bytes) | One membership state | Every member of that epoch; sealed to each with an ephemeral X25519 exchange against their member key |
| Epoch message key, confirm key | Derived from the secret with HKDF-SHA-256, salted by group id and epoch | Same |
| Edge parameters | The pair, for the life of the group | The two members only |

The committer generates a fresh secret for every commit (admission, removal, role transfer, rotation) and seals it to each member of the **new** roster. A removed member is sent the commit, so it learns it is out, without a secret. A new member's welcome carries the secret of the epoch that admits it and nothing earlier. Secrets of the last 32 epochs are kept (sixteen before revision 0.9) so a member can hand them on during catch-up; older epochs become unreadable.

The commit carries a **confirmation tag**: HMAC-SHA-256, under the epoch's confirm key, of the commit without its tag. A member that unseals a secret checks the tag before keeping it, so a member relaying a secret cannot hand out a wrong one, and the admin's signature covers the tag.

**What this gives and does not give.** Forward secrecy at the granularity of an epoch: a secret leaked later exposes that epoch. Post-compromise security through rotation: after removal or a rotate commit, whoever held the old secret and is not in the roster learns nothing new. A compromised **member seed** is not healed by rotation, because member keys are static: the member must be removed, and its edges with it. This is the deliberate simplification over MLS (see 900 § Security profile); it is adequate for 32 members and reviewable in a few hundred lines.

## Membership chain

A commit is `{ v, g, e, p, k, m, by, s?, ts, c, sig }`: version 1, group id, epoch (its index in the chain), the hash of the previous commit (empty for the genesis), a kind (`create`, `add`, `remove`, `role`, `rotate`), the whole roster after it as sorted `[key, role]` pairs, the committer, the member concerned, a timestamp, the confirmation tag and the committer's Ed25519 signature over the canonical tuple `[v, g, e, p, k, m, by, s, ts, c]`. The hash is SHA-256 of that same tuple.

Rules a receiver enforces, in this order: shape (at most 32 members, eight before revision 0.9; exactly one admin, sorted, distinct), group id, `e` is the next index, `p` matches, the committer is the admin of the previous roster, the roster is exactly the previous roster with the change applied (an admin cannot remove or demote itself; `rotate` names nobody), and the signature verifies. A chain is verified from its genesis; a chain is at most 1024 commits.

**Trust anchor.** The invitation arrives over the inviter's authenticated contact chat, naming the inviter's member key as the admin. The welcome's chain must admit the joiner in a commit signed by that key. Everything else about the roster is what the admin says: a member key with no owner never connects and never signs, but its presence in the list is the admin's claim.

**Forks.** Two validly signed commits after the same epoch halt the group on every member that sees both: status `forked`, no sending, no further commits, re-form the group. A bare claim (a `group-sync` naming a different hash) is not evidence; it is answered with the commit for that epoch, so only a validly signed conflicting history forks anyone.

## Admission and departure

```
inviter → contact:  { "t": "group-invite", "g", "name", "admin", "e", "n" }
contact → inviter:  { "t": "group-accept", "g", "key" }   |   { "t": "group-decline", "g" }
inviter → contact:  { "t": "group-chain", "g", "commits": [ … ] } *   (24 commits at a time, when the chain is long)
inviter → contact:  { "t": "group-welcome", "g", "name", "commits": [ … ], "secrets": [ { "e", "s": <sealed> } ] }
```

The invitee generates its member key on accepting. The admin commits `add`, tells the existing members over their edges, and sends the welcome over the contact chat. The joiner verifies the chain, unseals and confirms the secret, then opens edges to every other member. Being in the roster is the only admission; the contact chat is only the authenticated path that carried it.

### Entry link (`group-entry/1`)

An admin can also hand out **one link that anyone may use**, so people who are not their contacts can join, without a contact chat and without saying who they are:

```
group1/<group id>/<entry key>          (in the web app: https://…/#/join/group1/<group id>/<entry key>)
```

The **entry key** is an Ed25519 key the admin makes for the link and for nothing else; its seed stays on the admin's device. From the link alone every holder derives the same **knock identity** (seed = HKDF-SHA-256 over the entry key, salted by the group id, info `ghostly-group-entry/1 knock seed`) and a knock key (info `… knock key`).

1. The joiner generates its member key for the group and publishes, under the knock identity, a `_knock` record: the latest knocks `[[member key, ms], …]` (at most six, each fresh for three minutes), sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the knock key with the group id as associated data. It reads the record first and keeps other joiners' fresh knocks, since everyone holding the link writes the same Pkarr key; it reads the record back about a second after writing it (another joiner that wrote at the same moment may have replaced it; twice in a row at most, since a relay may still answer with the record from before), then every five seconds (every twenty after two minutes), and writes its knock again when it is gone from the record or thirty seconds old, until the admin's side of its entry session shows up, and again if that side goes away. Its app shows how far it got: knocking, knocked, answered (the admin's side is there), let in, then connecting to the members until the first edge is up.
2. The admin's app reads the knock identity every five seconds, and every two for ten minutes after the link was handed out or someone knocked (people open a link in the minutes after it is shared, and in bursts). For every fresh knock that is not a member, not already in progress and not recently timed out, while the group has room, it opens an **entry session**: a paired-chat/1 link whose rendezvous identities and discovery key derive, exactly like an edge, from the X25519 secret of the entry key and the joiner's member key, under the salt `entry/<group id>` so it never coincides with an edge. Each side pins the other in advance: the admin the knocked member key, the joiner the entry key. At most four run at once; one that does not finish within three minutes is closed, and its key is answered again after 30 seconds, then a minute, doubling up to ten minutes while its sessions keep timing out (a slow network is no refusal); an app that cannot follow the group is not answered for ten.
3. On the open session the ordinary admission runs: `group-invite`, then `group-accept` (sent at once: opening the link was the joiner's consent) whose `key` must be the member key the session is pinned to, then the welcome. The joiner closes its side when it has joined; the admin closes its own shortly after sending the welcome. Both sides of an entry session poll fast while it comes up (the other side is due any moment), and so do the admin and the new member on the edge between them, which they open right after; other edges come up at the ordinary pace. An entry session is never a contact chat: nobody records it as one, and courtesy notices do not use it.

**Trust anchor.** The entry key the link named. The session is pinned to it, so the admin who invites over it is whoever holds its seed, and the welcome must admit the joiner in a commit signed by the member key that invitation named, as for a contact's invitation.

**Who dials.** Of a paired session's two ends the lower rendezvous key dials, once it has seen the other end. The joiner draws its member key (two tries on average) so that its end of the entry session is the higher one: the admin's side, which opens the session when it reads the knock and finds the joiner already there, then puts its offer in its first packet, and the admission takes two trips through Pkarr (offer, answer) instead of three, as in the [community profile](9xx-group-community.md). Nothing on the wire says so; an admin's app from before behaves the same.

**Revocation.** The admin turns the link off, or replaces it with a new entry key; knocks under the old knock identity are then read by nobody. The link also stops working when its admin stops being the admin (the app turns it off): a new admin makes its own.

**What it gives and does not give.** The link is a bearer capability: whoever has it joins while it is on and the admin's app is open, as many people as the group has room for, and the admin cannot tell who they are beyond the name they announce. Relays see a Pkarr key and an opaque value, then ordinary paired signaling; neither the group id nor the member keys appear on them. Holders of the link can read each other's pending member keys and can overwrite each other's knocks (they are republished), which is a nuisance, not a way in. There is no approval step, expiry or use count in this increment; those are open decisions.

A member leaves by wiping its secrets at once and sending `{ "t": "group-leave", "g" }` to the admin, over their edge and over their contact chat when there is one, and the admin commits `remove`. The app drops the group and its history from the device at once; it keeps only a tombstone (the member key and the edge to that admin) until the admin's `remove` commit, or its `group-removed` on the contact chat, comes back, and repeats the leave whenever that edge opens, for at most a week. An admin with other members hands the role to one whose edge is up (a `role` commit) and then leaves like anyone else; with nobody reachable it cannot leave, since the role commit would reach no one. The last member leaving simply deletes the group. Removal is a `remove` commit; the removed member is told over its edge and, as a courtesy, with `{ "t": "group-removed", "g" }` over the contact chat when the remover has one. A removed member that was offline for both is told when it is back: the admin that removed it keeps its edge to that member, and when the edge opens sends the `remove` commit again, without a secret, and nothing else. A member further behind on the chain says where it is in its `group-sync`, and gets the commits between that epoch and its removal, without secrets, three times at most. Whatever else arrives on that edge is dropped, the removed member's name included, and the edge carries nothing else of the group or of the admin: no message, sync, statement, payment, reaction, pin, wake-up subscription, name or picture, whatever the member's app announces. It closes 15 seconds after the commit first went, and a minute at most after its connection opened whether the commit went or not, or after a week if the member never comes back; an admin waits for at most eight such members per group, the latest removed. Only the admin that made the commit tells, and it goes on telling after it hands the role on; the new admin does not, having no edge kept. A removed member whose admin's app is closed, or has left the group since, is not told; it sees the group go silent. A member invited again before it learned of its removal drops that invitation (its app still holds the group as active); once told, the next invitation is taken as any other.

**Current members only.** What a member says to the group goes over its edges to the members the roster has now, and over no other: messages, edits, reactions, pins, statements, payment requests and their results, payment notes, typing, wake-up subscriptions and what a hub says it reaches. An edge can outlive its member's place in the roster, for the moment before it closes or while it is kept as above; over such an edge go the commits up to the one that took the member out, and nothing after.

## Messages

```
{ "t": "group-msg", "g", "e", "s": <sender member key>, "n": <sequence in epoch>, "ts", "nn": <nonce>, "c": <box>, "sig", "xs"? }
```

Text is trimmed UTF-8 of at most 16 KiB, encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the epoch message key with the JSON of `[g, e, s, n, ts]` as associated data, then signed by the sender over `["ghostly-group/1 msg", g, e, s, n, ts, nn, c]`. Since revision 0.9 the sender also signs the whole frame, the boxes of the mentions (`m`) and the reply (`r`) included, as `xs` over `["ghostly-group/1 msg+", g, e, s, n, ts, nn, c, m.n, m.c, r.n, r.c]` (an absent box is two empty strings). `sig` stays what older apps check; `xs` is what lets another member hand the frame on with its boxes (§ Catch-up). Sequence numbers start at 0 in every epoch and are the sender's; the stable id of a message is `<sender>:<epoch>:<sequence>`.

A receiver accepts a frame from the edge of the member it names as sender, or handed on by another member (§ Catch-up); only for an epoch the sender and the receiver were both members of; only once per `(sender, epoch, sequence)`, remembering the highest sequence and the 256 below it per sender and epoch; and only with a valid signature and a ciphertext that opens. A frame for an epoch ahead of the receiver's chain, or one whose secret has not arrived, waits (64 frames, 1 MiB) while the receiver asks the sender to catch it up, at most once every ten seconds per member. Anything else is dropped without a reply.

## Mentions

A message may name members: "@Bob, are you in?" notifies Bob. Names can collide and change, so a mention is bound to the **member key**, not the name. The text keeps a readable `@Name` at each place, so an app without mentions shows the message as it was typed; a list says which member each place is:

```
[ { "k": <member key> | "*", "o": <offset>, "l": <length> }, … ]
```

`o` and `l` count Unicode code points of the (trimmed) text, `l` includes the `@`, and the text at `o` is `@`. A receiver keeps an entry only if its key is a member key (or `*`, below), its place is inside the text, starts with `@`, is 2 to 65 code points long and holds no line break, control or direction-changing character (#301), and it does not overlap an earlier one; it drops a bad entry on its own, and a list of more than **16** entries whole. The app shows each kept place as `@` and the member's name as it is when shown (the place's own text for someone no longer in the group), a mention of the reader stronger than the others.

**On the wire.** The list travels as JSON beside the text, in an optional field of `group-msg`:

```
{ "t": "group-msg", …, "sig", "m"?: { "n": <nonce>, "c": <box> } }
```

It is sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the same epoch message key, with a nonce of its own and the JSON of `["ghostly-group/1 mentions", g, e, s, n, ts]` as associated data, so it opens only on its own message. It is outside `sig`, which stays exactly the one older apps check. On the author's own edge, the edge vouches for the box, and a member who could tamper with it could only have written it. A frame handed on by another member keeps its boxes only when the author's `xs` covers them; otherwise the receiver drops `m` and `r` and takes the text (§ Catch-up). A box that does not open, or opens to something else, is no mentions; the message is taken all the same. The author keeps the box with the frame, so catch-up carries it.

**Everyone.** `*` names every member, and is kept only when the sender was the admin of the message's epoch; the composer offers `@everyone` to the admin only. A member's `*` is dropped by the sender's own app and by every receiver.

**Notifications.** A message that names the reader (or everyone) is a mention. It notifies as any message does. While the group is muted it still does, if the group's "Still notify me when I'm mentioned" is on, as it is unless turned off. That choice, like the mute, is the device's and is never sent. An unread mention puts an `@` beside the group in the chat list.

## Wake-up push

A member whose web app is closed misses a group until it opens by itself. Since revision 2026-09-28, a message that **names** the member wakes it, as a 1:1 message wakes a contact ([401 § Wake-up push](401-paired-chat.md#wake-up-push)): the same push subscription, the same encrypted push, the same "New message". Only a mention does: a group's every message would wake every member's phone.

**Sharing.** A member whose person turned "Wake me while closed" on shares its subscription with each other member on **their own edge**, under a token of that edge alone, as a group frame:

```
{ "t": "group-wake", "g": <group id>, "w": { "e", "p", "a", "vp", "vk", "k" } }
{ "t": "group-wake", "g": <group id>, "w": null }
```

`w` is exactly 401's `paired-wake` target, read with the same checks (an `https` push service on a public host name, a key pair whose halves match, a token of 16 to 64 base64url characters). It goes whenever the edge opens, and again when the subscription changes (a new subscription is a new token on every edge, as in chats). A receiver takes it only from the edge of the member it names, for that edge's group, at most 6 a minute per edge, and keeps it with the edge; a frame for another group, or a malformed one, changes nothing. An app without it drops the frame, as it drops any group frame it does not know. The edge is the pair's authenticated session, so nothing of it is handed on: no hub, no catch-up, no third member sees a member's subscription or token.

**Muted groups.** A group muted on this device is not woken at all: its members are told `w: null` on every edge (again each time an edge opens while it stays muted), and the edges' tokens are dropped; unmuted, each member gets a new token. A member may keep what it was told anyway, so muting a group whose members held tokens also replaces the subscription (a new endpoint, key pair and tokens), as muting a chat does ([401 § Wake-up push](401-paired-chat.md#wake-up-push)). "Still notify me when I'm mentioned" does not wake a closed app: it is about notices while the app runs. The push worker checks the mute too, for a member that was away when the group was muted.

**Waking.** When a message of mine names members by key (§ Mentions), each named member that shared a target, and that I cannot reach now (its edge is down and no hub reaches it), gets one push, `{"wake":1,"k":"<its token>"}`, with 401's encryption, VAPID signature, `TTL: 3600` and `Urgency: high`. Never for `*`: everyone is the whole roster, and a wake-up is for one person. Never for an edit, a reaction or a forwarded text, which carry no new mention. The limits, on the author's side, hold whatever the push service does:

- one push per member every **5 minutes**, whatever group names it; the count starts over once the member is reachable again;
- at most **4** pushes per group per **minute**: a message naming 16 members wakes four, and a burst of mentions wakes no more.

A push service that answers 404 or 410 forgets the target, as in chats. Nothing is retried: the message waits for the member on the other members' devices either way (§ Catch-up).

**Showing.** The woken app's push worker finds the token in its table (the group's route, its mute) and shows "New message", opening the group; notices of one group replace each other. A group has no calls: a push that claims to be one (`"c":1`) on a group's token is shown as a message.

**Privacy.** The push carries a token and nothing else: no text, no group name, no author, and the token means something only to the woken app. The push service learns what it learns of a 1:1 wake-up. A member's subscription reaches only the members it shares an edge with in a group it has not muted, which is the roster it already shares its edges with; each gets a token of its own, so two members cannot tell they woke the same app for the same group. A member it shared with can wake it (within its own app's limits, which a modified app need not keep) until the subscription is replaced. The app replaces it by itself when the group is muted, and when an edge whose member held a token closes because that member is no longer in the group (removed, or the group left), as it does for a deleted chat; an edge a group on hubs only stops keeping is still a member's, and replaces nothing then. That member still holds the subscription with no edge left to close, so since revision 2026-09-28 the app remembers it, and replaces the subscription when that member leaves the roster later, when this profile leaves or forgets the group, or when the group is muted. "New address" in Settings replaces it too.

**Not yet.** In a group on hubs (past 16 members), a member keeps edges with two hubs only, so it shares its subscription only with them and with members it had an edge with before; the others cannot wake it. Carrying a subscription through a hub needs a frame sealed to one member, as the community's pair payloads are, and is open. Communities have no edge between two members and do not wake anyone ([9xx · Group Community § Wake-up push](9xx-group-community.md#wake-up-push)).

## Replies

A message may answer an earlier message of the group ([400](400-chat.md#replies), revision 0.6): `{ "i": <message id>, "s": <line>, "f": <author's member key> }`, where the message id is `<sender key>:<epoch>:<seq>`. It travels like the mentions, sealed in an optional field of its own:

```
{ "t": "group-msg", …, "sig", "m"?: { "n", "c" }, "r"?: { "n": <nonce>, "c": <box> } }
```

under the epoch message key, with a nonce of its own and `["ghostly-group/1 reply", g, e, s, n, ts]` as associated data, outside the signature older apps check and inside `xs`, like the mentions. A box that does not open, or holds no valid reply (an `f` that is not a member key included), is no reply; the message is taken all the same. Replies and mentions go together: "@Bob yes" can answer Alice's message and name Bob. A member finds the original among the group's messages it has; an original from before it joined, or from an epoch it cannot read, is shown from the wire's line, marked as not found here.

## Forwards

A forwarded text ([400](400-chat.md#forwards), revision 0.12) carries `f`, its hop count (1 to 255), on the frame itself, in the clear like the header: every member reads it anyway, and it says nothing but a number.

```
{ "t": "group-msg", …, "sig", "m"?, "r"?, "f"?: 1..255, "xs"? }
```

Outside `sig`, which older apps check as it was; inside `xs`, after the boxes and only when present (`["ghostly-group/1 msg+", g, e, s, n, ts, nn, c, m.n, m.c, r.n, r.c, f]`), so what an older app signs and checks is unchanged. A member handing the frame on keeps `f` only with the author's `xs`, as the boxes; a frame handed on without it arrives without `f`. A receiver drops an `f` that is not such a number and takes the message. Older apps ignore it.

## Status cards

A bot's status card ([4xx · Status Cards](4xx-status-cards.md), revision 2026-09-29) rides in a sealed box of its own, `sc`, beside the text, which is its fallback: the card as JSON (at most 8 KiB), sealed under the message's epoch key with a nonce of its own, bound to the header (AAD `["ghostly-group/1 card", g, e, s, n, ts]`).

```
{ "t": "group-msg", …, "sig", "m"?, "r"?, "f"?, "sc"?: { "n", "c" }, "xs"? }
```

Outside `sig`; inside `xs` after everything else and only when present (`[…, r.n, r.c, f?, "sc", sc.n, sc.c]`), so what an older app signs and checks for other messages is unchanged. A member handing the frame on keeps the box only with the author's `xs`; a copy handed on without it arrives as text, and a whole copy completes it (unless an edit came meanwhile: a card belongs to its version). A box that does not open, or a card that does not hold, is no card: the text shows.

A `group-edit`'s sealed body MAY carry the card of that version, `{ "text", "m"?, "sc"? }`, and with one its `v` may go up to **5,000**; a `v` past 100 without a card is dropped. An older member drops an edit numbered past 100 and keeps the last text it took.

## Reactions

A member reacts to a group message ([400](400-chat.md#reactions), revision 0.7) with a frame of its own, sent by the member who reacts over each of its edges:

```
{ "t": "group-react", "g": <group id>, "id": <message id>, "e": <emoji or "">, "n": <number> }
```

It is believed only from the member the edge is pinned to, and only while that member is in the roster: the edge authenticates it, so it needs no signature of its own. Reactions are not in the message log and not handed on in a catch-up: a member hears again the reactor's latest reactions (up to 32) when their edge opens. They are not sealed under the epoch key: an edge is already a session between exactly those two members. In a group on hubs, a reaction also carries the reactor's signature, and hubs pass it on as `group-reacted` (§ Hubs).

## Pinned message

Any member may pin a message of the group, or unpin it ([400](400-chat.md#pinned-message): one per group, the last pin wins). The pin is signed by its pinner, so that any member can pass it on as it is:

```
{ "t": "group-pin", "g": <group id>, "id": <message id or "">, "n": <number>, "k": <pinner's member key>, "sig": <Ed25519 over ["ghostly-group/1 pin", g, id, n]> }
```

A member takes it when `k` is in the roster, the signature holds, `n` is at most 5 minutes past its clock ([400](400-chat.md#pinned-message)) and the rule allows `k` (any member here), whichever edge it came over. A pin goes with its pinner: when `k` leaves the roster, each member drops the pin it shows by `k`, and the group has none until the next one. When it is newer than the pin it shows, it keeps it (with `k` and `sig`) and sends it on over its other edges, so a member with no edge to the pinner (a group on hubs) gets it too; an older pin is dropped there, which ends the passing on. Every member says the group's pin again over an edge when it opens, so a member who was away gets it then. Pins are not in the message log. Apps from before drop the frame: its `t` is one they do not know.

## Edits

The author of a text can edit it ([400](400-chat.md#edits), revision 0.8): the whole new text, with an edit number of its own per message (1 for the first, at most 100; the highest wins, whatever order edits arrive in). It goes over each of the author's edges as a frame of its own:

```
{ "t": "group-edit", "g", "e": <the message's epoch>, "s": <author>, "n": <the message's sequence>, "v": <edit number>, "ts", "nn": <nonce>, "c": <box>, "sig" }
```

The message it edits is `<s>:<e>:<n>`. The box holds `{ "text": <new text>, "m"?: <mentions> }` as JSON, sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the message key of **the message's epoch** `e`, with `["ghostly-group/1 edit", g, e, s, n, v, ts]` as associated data; the author signs `["ghostly-group/1 edit", g, e, s, n, v, ts, nn, c]`. Sealing under the message's epoch means an edit reaches only who could read the message: the author sends it to the members of epoch `e` still in the roster, and someone admitted later could not open it. A message whose epoch secret the author no longer keeps (past the last 32 epochs, sixteen before revision 0.9) cannot be edited.

A receiver takes an edit from the edge of the member it names as `s`, or handed on by another member (below), only while the author is **in the current roster** (a removed member's edits are dropped, even of messages it sent while a member), only for an epoch the author and the receiver were both members of, with a valid signature and a box that opens to a text within the bounds of a message. It files it under the author's own message only; one for a message not here yet waits a minute for it (32 per group), and one for an epoch or a secret not here yet waits for it as a message does. The new text's mentions are checked as a message's (`*` only from the admin of epoch `e`); an edit that names a member is not a new mention and does not notify. A receiver takes at most 30 edits from one member in 10 seconds.

**Handed on (revision 0.10).** As with messages ([Catch-up](#catch-up)), any member hands on the edits of a member whose edge to someone is down. Each member keeps the latest edit it took of each other member's message, as it arrived and with only the fields of an edit frame: 64 edits and 512 KiB, the oldest going first, and none of a member once it is out of the roster. A sync whose `ask` names an author is answered, after the messages, with the latest 16 kept edits of that author for epochs the asker was a member of; a sync says nothing of edits, so they go whether or not the asker has them, and one it has changes nothing. A receiver takes an edit handed on only while both the member handing it on and its author are in its roster; the author's signature covers the whole frame, so a member handing an edit on can withhold it, never change or forge it.

There are no receipts. The author says each edit once to the edges that are up, at most 10 in 10 seconds per group (an edit made while an earlier one waits replaces it), and says its latest 16 edits again to a member whose edge opens, which covers a member who was away; a member whose edge to the author stays down gets them from whoever holds them. An edit that is not newer than what is shown changes nothing.

## Typing

A member's app shows who else is writing ("Ana is typing…", "Ana and Bo are typing…", "3 people are typing…"), recording a voice note or thinking (revision 2026-09-28), with the kinds, status line, pace and timeout of a 1:1 chat ([401](401-paired-chat.md#typing)). The writer sends a frame of its own over each of its edges that is up, to members in the roster:

```
{ "t": "group-typing", "g": <group id>, "e": <epoch>, "n": <nonce>, "c": <box> }
```

The box holds the 1:1 frame's word without its `t`, as JSON (`{"s":"start"}`, `{"s":"start","kind":"thinking","status":"Reading the thread"}`, `{"s":"stop"}`), sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the message key of epoch `e`, with `["ghostly-group typing", g, e, <writer's member key>]` as associated data. One box goes to every edge. It is sealed although an edge is already a session between two members, so that only a member of that epoch can read it, and bound to the writer so that a member cannot replay another's frame on its own edge. It is presence, not a message: no id, no sequence number, no signature, never stored, never in the chain, never in a sync or a catch-up, never handed on and never passed on by a hub.

A receiver believes it only from the member its edge is pinned to, only while that member is in the roster and not itself, and only when the box opens under a secret it holds for epoch `e`; anything else is dropped unread, and so is a box longer than 512 characters. The word is then read as a 1:1 `paired-typing` frame: an unknown kind is plain typing, and a status is made one line, cleaned of invisible and direction-changing characters, cut to 40 characters and dropped when it holds a link or markup. Each member's word counts on its own: shown from a `start` until its `stop`, a message from that member, or 6 seconds without a new `start`, with at most 8 frames from one member in 10 seconds (a member who floods is held where it was; nobody else is). A member the roster takes out is no longer shown. The writer keeps the 1:1 pace: a `start` at most every 3 seconds (at once for a new kind or status), at most 6 frames in 10 seconds, and a `stop` when the text is cleared or sent. The app shows the plain words of the kind all members share, plain typing when they differ, and one member's status line after their name. A profile that turned `sendTyping` off says nothing in groups either.

In a group on hubs, typing reaches only the members the writer has an edge with: hubs do not pass it on.

## Catch-up

When an edge opens, each side sends what it knows:

```
{ "t": "group-sync", "g", "e", "h": <hash of my top commit>, "have": { <sender>: { <epoch>: <highest seq> } }, "secrets": [ <epochs I hold> ],
  "miss"?: { <sender>: { <epoch>: [ <seq>, … ] } }, "ask"?: [ <member key>, … ] }
```

The side that is ahead answers with the commits the other lacks (each with the secret sealed for the other when it was in that epoch's roster and the secret is still held), `{ "t": "group-secrets", "g", "secrets": [ … ] }` for epochs the other was in but does not hold (at most sixteen to a frame, what apps from before revision 0.9 take), and its **own** messages the other lacks, from its bounded log and only for epochs the other was a member of. A member behind on the chain asks in turn, and answers too with its own messages the other lacks, for the epochs of its own chain the other was a member of: what it sent before it heard of the newer commits (a joiner's first message, written before its first edge opened and while the admin admitted someone else) would otherwise wait until the edge opened another time, since nobody asks it again once it has caught up. It hands on nothing for other members while behind: whom to hand on for is the newer roster's to say.

**Any member hands on (revision 0.9).** A message whose author's edge to me is down can come from anyone else who received it:

1. **What is kept.** Each member keeps the frames of other members it accepted, as they arrived and with only the fields of a message frame: 256 frames and 256 KiB, the oldest going first. When a commit takes someone out of the roster, their frames leave the log.
2. **What is asked.** A sync may name members in `ask`: those whose edges to the sender are down. The receiver answers with the frames of those members it keeps that the sender lacks: above the sender's `have` for that sender and epoch, or listed in its `miss` (sequence numbers below the highest that never arrived, the newest 32 per sender and epoch). Only for epochs the sender was a member of, and only for members in both rosters.
3. **Who is asked.** When an edge opens, the app asks that member for every unreachable member not already asked of another member whose edge is still up. So a member whose edges come up one after another gets each missed message about once, not from everyone. While any member is unreachable, the app also sends one sync with `ask` to one connected member, in turn, every 60 seconds: a message sent while its author could reach that member and not me arrives within that, without any edge opening again. Each member starts its turns at a place of its own, from its key: members cut off from the same member (a Mac past its budget, § Hubs) would otherwise ask the same ones in step, all cut off too, for minutes. Spread out, some of them ask a member that has the message, and the others then get it from them.
4. **What a receiver takes.** A frame handed on (its sender is not the member at the other end of the edge) is taken only while both that member and its author are in the receiver's roster, with the same checks as any frame (epoch membership, replay window, signature, box). Its mention and reply boxes are kept only when the author's `xs` verifies; otherwise they are dropped and the text is taken. Since revision 2026-09-28 such a copy (handed on without an `xs` that verifies) is **provisional**: its text is shown, but it is not marked seen, not kept and not handed on, so the next sync still asks for it and a second provisional copy changes nothing. A whole copy (from its author's edge, or handed on with `xs`) then completes it: the mentions, the reply and the hop count join the message already shown, and only then is it seen. The last 256 provisional messages are saved with the group's state, so this holds across a restart. A member handing a copy on can withhold what `xs` covers, never make it stick.

The author re-sends from its own log as before, and fills `miss` too. Gaps that nobody who is asked still holds are reported per sender (`missing`), not hidden.

**How often a sync is answered (revision 2026-09-28).** An answer may carry commits, secrets and two logs, so a member answers at most 8 syncs from one member a minute; one past that is dropped unanswered. An edge opening, a question at most every ten seconds and a gossip turn a minute fit well within it.

**What it gives and does not give.** A member who comes back gets what it missed from whoever is there and holds it, not only from authors who are also back: a bot that posts and stops is still read by a member whose app opens later. Handing on adds no trust: frames are signed by their authors and sealed under epoch keys, so a member handing them on can only withhold them, never read more than it could already or forge. Someone removed gets no frame of a later epoch (it holds no secret for it, and it is in nobody's roster), and nothing it sent is handed on after its removal, even for epochs it was in. There is still no store for a member who never meets anyone holding what it missed: the log is bounded, and it lives only on members' devices.

## Hubs

Past 16 members a full mesh costs every member one edge per other member (§ Cost per member): at 32, 31 connections and a good part of the relays' budget, and a message costs its author one copy per member. Since revision 2026-09-28, members whose apps stay online carry a large group instead: they are its **hubs**, the other members keep edges with two of them, and the hubs pass everything on.

**Not a community.** The [community profile](9xx-group-community.md) already has hubs, and making a large private group "a community without a public link" was weighed and not chosen. A community is another session: any member admits, its commits are its own, the secret its rendezvous derives from comes with its link, nobody is invited from their contacts, and payments travel through the group. A private group would have to be converted, and would lose the one-admin chain, invitations from contacts and payments between two members over their own edge. So a private group keeps everything in this profile and borrows only the community's **topology**: its sealed beacon, its lobbies and its rendezvous ranking ([`communityRendezvous.ts`](../../packages/core/src/communityRendezvous.ts)), the same records under keys of its own ([`groupHubs.ts`](../../packages/core/src/groupHubs.ts), [`meshHubs.ts`](../../packages/browser/src/engine/meshHubs.ts)).

**When.** Up to 16 members a group is a full mesh, whatever hubs there could be. Past 16, it runs on hubs while a hub is known (listed in the beacon, or seen there in the last three minutes); otherwise it stays a full mesh (§ No hub, below).

**Who is a hub.** A member may be a hub when its app stays online (the Desktop app and the CLI say so; a browser tab does not) or the admin pinned it, and the admin did not exclude it. Such a member lists itself in the beacon while the beacon holds fewer than eight hubs, or always when pinned. There is no election beyond that: the members that stay online are few, and a hub that is not needed costs only itself.

**Budget.** An app may give its groups a budget of WebRTC connections. The Desktop app on a Mac gives them 40: WKWebView opens only about 46 in one page, and past that a new connection gathers no candidate, so a call or a 1:1 chat over WebRTC does not connect (§ Cost per member). Such an app is a hub of a group only while an edge with every other member fits beside what its other groups and its 1:1 chats over WebRTC hold, where a group it is already a hub of counts in full; pinned or not, since a hub past its budget could not keep an edge with the members that pick it. Otherwise it does not list itself, or steps down, and its members move to another hub; with none, the group is a full mesh. Whatever the group runs on, the app opens only the edges its room allows, the most useful first: the hubs it counts on, the admin, members it just met, edges already up, then the others in key order. What the members it has no edge with send reaches it through those (§ Catch-up). Other apps have no budget: WebKitGTK (Linux) has no WebRTC in the page, and WebView2 (Windows) has no ceiling that low.

**The admin's choice.** The admin pins members as hubs and excludes others in the group's metadata (§ Metadata), beside the picture: the body becomes `{ "pic"?, "hubs"?: { "pin": [<member key>, …], "no": [<member key>, …] } }`, at most 8 pinned and 32 excluded, none both. It is the admin's signed word and is handed on like the picture. A policy that does not hold is no policy, and the picture beside it still shows. Every member ignores an excluded member listed in the beacon, so one that lists itself anyway is nobody's hub. A change of hubs alone leaves no line in the group's history. In the app, the admin's members panel offers each member "Hub if always on", "Always a hub" or "Never a hub"; on the CLI, `ghostly group hub <group> <member> --pin | --exclude | --auto`.

**Where hubs are found.** In two ways: from the beacon, and from the hubs themselves over any edge (`group-reach`, below). The beacon is as in the community (9xx · Group Community § Topology), with one difference: the secret the records derive from. A private group's is the **epoch's rendezvous secret**, HKDF-SHA-256 of the epoch secret, salted by `<group id>/<epoch>`, with the info `ghostly-group-mesh/1 rendezvous`. From it come the beacon (the hubs, how many members each holds and since when, sealed under a Pkarr identity every member of the epoch derives) and one lobby per hub (the members asking that hub for an edge), exactly as the community writes them. A hub republishes its entry every 30 seconds; a member reads the beacon once a minute; all of it is background requests. The records move with every commit: a hub writes the new epoch's beacon as soon as it has applied the commit, and a hub seen in the last three minutes still counts meanwhile.

**Edges.**

- A member keeps edges with **two hubs**, picked by the community's rendezvous rank for its key among the fresh hubs: members spread over the hubs, and a hub that goes moves only its own members. Hubs holding fewer than 48 members come first, a full one rather than none (it keeps an edge with everyone anyway, so it is only a busier one), and a hub that republished lately before one that may be gone; one that has not taken the member within a minute, or whose edge dropped, is avoided for two. The member writes its key in each hub's lobby every 20 seconds until that hub's edge is up.
- A hub keeps an edge with **every** member, as in a full mesh; the lobby makes it look fast for the members that asked there. So a member that could not read the beacon (its relays' budget spent on the edges of a full mesh, just before hubs came) still reaches the hubs, and learns from them that they are hubs (**Presence**, below). Members whose apps take no hubs are served the same way (§ Compatibility).
- The admin and a member it admits keep their edge for two minutes, hubs or not: the welcome and the first sync go over it.
- An app that just started opens the edges it has and the hubs it used last (it records them), and waits for its first reading of the beacon, at most 30 seconds, before opening any other: a large group does not open every edge at once.
- An edge paces its Pkarr reads by how many edges its member keeps (`meshEdgeIntervals`), not by the roster: a member with two edges looks at them as a chat does.

**Passing on.** A hub passes on, to each of its other edges that is up, what its session took for the first time: messages, edits, commits (without the secret sealed for the hub), metadata statements and signed leaves; never back to the edge it came on, nor to its author. A receiver takes each as a frame handed on (§ Catch-up): only while both the hub and the author are in its roster, checked against the author's signature, its boxes only under `xs`. A frame of someone the chain took out is not passed on. Only a first copy goes on, so a flood among hubs stops at every member that already has it: a member with two hubs takes each message twice and keeps it once.

- **Secrets.** A commit a hub passes on carries no secret for the receiver. A member that applies a commit and holds no secret for the new epoch asks whoever handed it the commit, at once, with a sync; that member seals the secret to it (§ Catch-up). The admin keeps a direct edge with members whose apps take no hubs, which would otherwise wait for a message before asking.
- **Leaving.** `group-leave` is believed from the edge it comes on, and a member may have no edge with the admin. A member leaving a group on hubs also sends `{ "t": "group-bye", "g", "k": <its member key>, "e", "ts", "sig" }`, signed over `["ghostly-group/1 bye", g, k, e, ts]`; hubs pass it on, and the admin removes `k` while it is in the roster. The tombstone keeps the edges to those hubs and says it again when one opens; the admin's commit that removes it may come back through a hub.
- **Reactions.** A reaction said on the reactor's own edges now carries the reactor's member key and signature, `k` and `sig` over `["ghostly-group/1 react", g, id, e, n]`; older apps read only the reaction's fields. A hub passes a signed one on as a frame of its own, `{ "t": "group-reacted", "g", "k", "id", "e", "n", "sig" }`, which older apps drop (they would take it as the hub's own reaction), once per member, message and number, and says its latest 64 again to a member whose edge opens.
- **Presence.** A hub tells its edges whom it reaches, `{ "t": "group-reach", "g", "k": [<member key>, …], "l"?: [<member key>, …] }`, when that changes (at most every five seconds), to an edge that just opened, and once a minute anyway. Only hubs say it: a member that hears it counts the sender as a hub (unless the admin excluded it), as a reading of the beacon would. A member shows another it has no edge with as reachable "through a hub" while a hub whose edge is up lists it. `l` names members whose apps take no hubs.

**Catch-up** is § Catch-up unchanged: when an edge opens, and in the 60-second gossip turn, a member asks one connected member (a hub, now) for what the members it has no edge with sent.

**No hub.** With no hub in the beacon and none seen for three minutes, members fall back to the full mesh and the 60-second gossip turn: nothing is lost, the group costs what it costs without hubs, and it goes back to hubs when one lists itself again.

**Removed members.** As for the community's admin changes (#300): the commit that removes a member moves the beacon and the lobbies to a rendezvous secret that member does not get; a hub keeps edges and takes lobby and beacon entries only for members of its roster; its edge to the removed member closes 15 seconds after the commit, the commit that tells it being the only thing passed on to it meanwhile; and its frames are neither passed on nor taken from a hub, even for epochs it was in (§ Catch-up). A hub the admin removes passes on the commit that removes it, and closes its edges 15 seconds later: the admin may have had no other edge, and when it was the last hub, the members fall back to the full mesh as soon as they apply it.

**What it gives and does not give.**

- A hub sees who talks when, how often and how long each message is, and who is reachable. It reads nothing a member does not already read, since it is one. It can withhold what passes through it: each member keeps two hubs, and the gossip turn fills a gap from any member that has what is missing.
- One more hop: most members get a message from a hub, not from its author. With two hubs each message crosses the network about twice as often as in a full mesh, and a hub sends one copy per member; the author sends two.
- A hub costs what a member of the full mesh costs: an edge with every member. That is why hubs are apps that stay online and read the DHT (§ Cost per member), not browser tabs, unless the admin pins one.
- Not carried by hubs yet: payments between two members, which need their own edge, and payment notes (`group-pay`), which are believed from the edge of the member they are about. In a group on hubs they reach only members that share an edge (§ Open decisions).

## Payments

A payment in a group is a payment **between two members**, carried by the edge between them, plus a **note** the rest of the group sees. Nothing about the money ever crosses a third member. In a group on hubs (§ Hubs) two members share an edge only when one of them is a hub or on an older app, so a payment between two other members has no edge to travel on yet (§ Open decisions).

**On the edge.** An edge is a paired-chat/1 link ([401](401-paired-chat.md)): it offers and negotiates the ways of paying exactly as a contact chat does ([200](200-payments.md), `paired-payments`), and carries the ordinary frames (`pay-ask`, `pay-req`, `pay`, `pay-res`). So a member pays or asks another member on any rail both of their apps allow, through the same review and explicit approval as a chat; the receipts and the reclaim of unredeemed ecash are the chat's. An app whose edges offer no payments (older ones) negotiates none, and the other side says it needs an update.

**A request to the whole group** (`f` = `*`) is one request, with one id, sent as a `pay-req` on every edge (and again to a member whose edge opens later), that any member may pay **once**:

1. **One rail.** Cashu *or* Lightning, never both, because each enforces "once" on its own and two together could each be paid. A Lightning request carries one invoice: an invoice is paid at most once. A Cashu request names the payee's mints.
2. **First valid payment wins.** The payee's app handles the tokens for one request one after the other. Before redeeming a token it checks that the request is still open and that the token would settle it (amount, mint); if not, it answers `pay-res` with `ok: false` and **does not redeem it**, so the payer's app takes its ecash back as for any refused payment. The first token that is redeemed settles the request.
3. **Everyone's copy closes.** When the request settles (a token redeemed, or the payee's own wallet seeing the invoice paid), the payee sends `pay-res` `{ id: <request id>, ok: true }` on every edge; a member's copy of the request is then paid and cannot be paid again. A member whose edge was down gets that receipt when it opens.
4. **Idempotent receipts.** A token sent again is answered from what was recorded (redeemed once, never twice); a receipt for a request already closed changes nothing; a refused token is refused again.

**The note.** Each side tells the group about its own part, on every edge:

```
{ "t": "group-pay", "g", "id", "k": "req" | "pay", "f": <payer key> | "*", "to": <payee key>,
  "v": <amount, smallest unit>, "u": <unit>, "d": <decimals>, "r": "cashu" | "lightning" | "arkade" | "bark" | "bitcoin" | "usdt",
  "x"?: 1 (test money), "m"?: <memo, 140 chars>, "a"?: 1 (answers the payer's own ask), "ts", "st": "open" | "sent" | "paid" | "closed", "by"?: <payer key> }
```

A receiver takes a note only from the member it is about, as authenticated by the edge it came on, and only when every key it names is in the roster: a request's state (`open`, `paid`, `closed`) and a payment's `paid` from the **payee**; `sent` (and `closed`, taking it back when its payment came back) from the **payer**, about itself (`by` = the author). The payee's description of a request, and the payer's of a payment, win over anyone else's; states only move forward and `paid` is final. For an invoice the payee's wallet cannot tell who paid: when exactly one member said `sent`, that member is named; otherwise the note says only that it was paid. Each device keeps the latest note it said per payment and says it again when an edge opens (its last 32). A note is information, never proof: the payee's wallet is the only thing that marks a request paid, as in a chat.

**What it gives and does not give.** The group sees who paid whom, how much, over what and a memo, and nothing to pay with (no invoice, address or token). Members see each other's amounts: payments in a group are not private from the group. A member's note is its own word; a lying payer can claim `sent`, and the request stays open until the payee's wallet says otherwise. A request to the group on an irreversible rail (Ark, Bark, on-chain, USDT) is not offered, since nothing could refuse a second payment.

## Metadata

A group has **metadata** besides its roster: its name and its picture. It is not in the membership chain: a new commit kind or field would make every older app refuse the chain. It travels in a frame of its own, which older apps drop, and it is **the admin's word**: only the admin sets or removes it, and every member keeps it and hands it on.

```
{ "t": "group-meta", "g", "e": <epoch of the commit signed under>, "h": <its hash>, "r": <revision>, "by": <admin key>, "ts",
  "d": <SHA-256 of the body>, "sig", "k": <epoch whose message key seals the body>, "nn": <nonce>, "c": <box> }
```

**The statement.** The admin signs `["ghostly-group meta", g, e, h, r, by, ts, d]` with its member key. `(e, h)` is a commit of the chain the signer is the admin after; `r` counts up from 1 under that commit, and starts again at 1 under a later one, never from a revision signed under an earlier commit (a former admin that signed the highest revision a member accepts would otherwise leave every later admin with one no member takes). `d` is the SHA-256 (hex) of the **body**, the metadata as JSON: `{ "name": <text>, "pic": <data URL> }`. The body is the whole metadata: a field left out is unset, so `{}` removes the picture and gives the group back its first name (below). An admin changing one field signs the others again as they are: a rename keeps the picture and the hubs, a new picture keeps the name. Unknown fields are ignored.

**The box.** The body is encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under the message key of epoch `k`, with the JSON of `["ghostly-group meta", g, k, d]` as associated data. The signature covers the body's hash, not the box, so any member can seal the same statement again under an epoch the recipient holds. Someone taken out of the group gets no later epoch key, so it cannot read a picture set after its removal.

**The picture** is what a profile picture may be ([the chat session's `paired-avatar`](401-paired-chat.md)): a `data:image/jpeg;base64,` URL whose JPEG frame header declares at most 512×512, never a URL to fetch. It is also at most 40,000 characters, so the sealed frame stays within the 60 KiB an edge carries. Ghostly makes it by cropping the chosen image to its centre square and redrawing it at 128×128 as a fresh JPEG (a few KB, with no metadata of the original file). A body with anything else (a URL, another format, larger dimensions) is refused whole.

**The name** is one line a member can trust to read as it looks. Any run of whitespace (a newline, a tab, a line or paragraph separator) becomes one space; control, format and other invisible characters are dropped, except the two joiners emoji and some scripts need (U+200C, U+200D), so direction overrides cannot reorder it; it is trimmed. What is left must be 1 to 64 characters (code points) and at most 256 bytes of UTF-8, or it is not a name: the admin's app refuses it, and a receiver ignores that field (the rest of the body, the picture, still counts) rather than cutting it into another name. Every member cleans a received name the same way before showing it.

**The first name.** The chain does not carry a name. A group's creator names it, and a joiner learns the name from its invitation (`group-invite`) and its welcome (`group-welcome`), which say the name the group has at that moment, renamed or not. That **first name** is what a member keeps as the group's name of its own. The name members see is the metadata's `name` when the statement they hold has one, and their first name otherwise: before the statement reaches them, after a body without a name, and on apps from before names, which drop `name` with the other unknown fields. A body without a name therefore gives each member back its own first name, which for members who joined after a rename is that name. The metadata never changes the first name, so a statement that goes away takes nothing else with it.

**Which statement a member keeps.** A statement is taken only if all of these hold:

1. It comes from a member's edge.
2. It is **newer** than the one held: signed under a later commit, or a higher revision under the same one.
3. Its commit `h` is on the member's chain at index `e`.
4. Its signer was the admin after that commit **and is the admin now**.
5. The signature holds.
6. It opens under epoch `k` and hashes to `d`.
7. Its body is one Ghostly shows.

A statement naming a commit or epoch the member does not have yet waits (one at a time), while the member asks the sender to catch it up. A statement already taken is kept when the admin changes. Condition 4 means a former admin cannot overrule the new one by signing under a commit from before its demotion. Because of it, **a new admin signs the current metadata again**, under its own commit (revision 1), as soon as it applies the commit that made it admin, so members who join afterwards (who accept only the current admin's statement) still get it.

**Delivery.** The admin sends the frame to every member when it renames the group or sets or removes the picture; the same body again is no new statement. Sync frames carry `"mt": "<e>.<r>"` (`""` for none): the statement the sender holds. The side holding a newer one answers with it, sealed under its current epoch, after the commits and secrets the other lacks. That is how a member who was away, and a member admitted later, by a contact or by the link, gets the picture without the admin. The picture is shown after admission: a link carries nothing that would let a joiner fetch it before. An app from before metadata sends no `mt` and is sent nothing; it keeps showing the group glyph and its first name.

Each change leaves an event line in the group's history ("Alice changed the group's picture", "Alice renamed the group to “Book club”"); a statement that changes only the hubs leaves none. System notifications stay as they are, with no group name or picture: they say only that something arrived.

## Transports

A group's links (its edges, and the entry sessions of its link, § Entry link) go over WebRTC between two apps that have it, as they always did. An app with no WebRTC (Ghostly Desktop on Linux: WebKitGTK has none) reaches its members over a native transport instead, [Iroh](102-iroh.md) or [HyperDHT](103-hyperdht.md), with the same pinned [paired-chat/1](401-paired-chat.md) session on top. A 1:1 chat learns how to dial a contact's native transports from its [capability record](03-capabilities.md#layer-0-capability-record); a group's link has none, so its own packet says it:

- **`_tr`**, a label of the link's packet ([records](../../packages/core/src/records.ts)), sealed with the link key like the others: `{"t": transports, "d": descriptors}`. `t` lists the layer-1 transports the app runs on this link, `d` how to dial its native ones, in the capability record's form (the Iroh endpoint id and the relay it is reached through, the HyperDHT key; never an address).
- **Who publishes it.** An app with no WebRTC starts its native endpoints on every group link, and publishes `_tr` once one is up. An app that has WebRTC starts native endpoints on a link only when the member's `_tr` lists no `webrtc/1`, and then publishes its own `_tr`. Two apps that both have WebRTC publish none while WebRTC connects, so their packets, and a group of eight's listener count, are what they were.
- **An edge whose WebRTC connects nothing.** A network that lets no direct connection through (a VPN, a firewall, on either side) leaves an edge between two apps with WebRTC with nothing to go live on: there is no TURN server unless someone set one. An app whose WebRTC attempt on an edge exchanged both descriptions and connected nothing, or gathered no candidate at all, treats that edge as an app with no WebRTC does, until it restarts: it starts its native endpoints there and publishes `_tr` with no `webrtc/1`. The member's app follows by the rule above, and the edge goes live over Iroh through its relay. Entry sessions are not covered yet.
- **Dialling.** A `_tr` without `webrtc/1` withdraws a WebRTC offer out to that member (it is never answered), and the lower key dials the transports both run, as on a 1:1 chat ([100](100-transports.md)). A side with nothing to dial yet looks at the member's packet fast for its `_tr`. A member's transports are kept, so after a restart the edge starts its endpoints at once and resumes on a native transport both apps run.
- **Compatibility.** Apps from before ignore `_tr` (unknown labels are skipped), and stay WebRTC only: a member with no WebRTC is unreachable from them, as before. An app with no transport for a group's links at all (no WebRTC and no native transport) says so, and makes no group whose link nobody could use.
- **Cost.** Every native listener a group's link holds counts toward the app's native limit (eight per transport on the Desktop), shared with 1:1 chats. A group's links hold at most four of them, all groups together, and never take one a chat holds; a chat that needs one takes it back from a group link that carries no session. A link with none waits, its member's row says so, and it tries again as one frees up. With no WebRTC, those four are the app's budget of connections for groups (§ Hubs, Budget), and it does not offer to be a private group's hub.

## Compatibility

Groups are announced after the paired handshake, on the open session, as `{ "t": "paired-groups", "v": [1] }` (`[1, 2, 3, 4]` today: 2 is the [community profile](9xx-group-community.md), 3 says the app takes rosters past eight, 4 that it runs hubs), the way `paired-payments` is, so a full handshake offer stays within the sixteen capabilities older apps accept. An app without groups drops the announcement and every `group-*` frame (they carry no `id`), keeps chatting 1:1, and is shown as needing an update to be invited. No 1:1 behavior changes. Payments on edges came after groups (revision 0.3): an app from before offers none on its edges, drops `group-pay`, and simply sees no payments; nothing else changes for it. Metadata came in revision 0.4: an app from before drops `group-meta`, leaves `mt` out of its syncs and is sent none, and its chain rules are unchanged. Mentions came in revision 0.5: an app from before ignores `m` (it checks the fields it knows, and the signature covers only those), shows the `@Name` as text and notifies as for any message. Replies came in revision 0.6 and are ignored the same way (`r`). Reactions came in revision 0.7: an app from before drops `group-react` (a `t` its session does not know) and shows none. Edits came in revision 0.8 and are dropped the same way (`group-edit`): a member on an older app keeps showing the original text. An app of revision 0.8 or 0.9 drops an edit handed on to it (its sender is not the edge's member) and hands none on; it gets the author's edits when their edge opens. Revision 0.9 raised the roster to 32 and made catch-up any member's: an app from before refuses a commit whose roster holds more than eight as malformed and would stop following the group, so an admin grows a group past eight only when every other member's app announced version 3 (on its edge, or on the contact chat or entry session that admitted it) and the newcomer's does too; the app says who has not been seen updated. An app from before sends no `ask`, so it is handed nothing by third members, and it drops frames handed on to it (their sender is not the edge's member); it ignores `xs` and `miss`. Secrets go at most sixteen to a frame, as it takes them. Hubs came in revision 2026-09-28 and are announced as version 4 (`[1, 2, 3, 4]`). An app of revision 0.9 to 0.12 keeps every group a full mesh: it opens every edge, the hubs and the admin keep edges with it (every member records whose edge came up without version 4, and hubs say it on in `group-reach`), and it takes what hubs pass on as frames handed on, which it accepts. It drops `group-bye`, `group-reach` and `group-reacted`, and reads a reaction's fields without its signature; its own leave goes to the admin over their edge or their contact chat, as before. While a group runs on hubs, the admin lets in only newcomers whose apps announce version 4: hubs would not know to keep an edge with an older one. An older admin that sets the picture signs a body without `hubs`, and the policy is gone until an admin on a newer app sets it again. Names came in revision 2026-09-28 as a `name` field of the metadata: an app from before ignores it and shows its first name, and an admin on such an app that sets the picture signs a body without `name`, which gives every member its first name back until an admin on a newer app renames the group again. Typing came in revision 2026-09-28: an app from before drops `group-typing` (a `t` its session does not know) and shows no one typing; it needs no announcement.

The member cap is what the harness below measured (§ Cost per member); the rest are the first profile's numbers, chosen to be small.

## Security and privacy

- The admin sees, and can invent, the roster; it cannot read edges it is not part of, forge another member's messages or hand a removed member a new secret it does not have.
- Every member can read every message of every epoch it was in, keep it, and forward it out of band. Removal changes keys, not the past.
- Edges are pairwise Pkarr links: relays see one rendezvous key per member per edge and its signaling; the group id and the roster never touch the DHT.
- A member's device holds its member seed, the chain, the secrets of recent epochs and its own recent messages in the clear at rest, as chats are held today. Profile backups include them.
- Denial of service by an insider is bounded to what one edge can carry; a validly signed fork by the admin halts the group by design.
- In a group on hubs, a hub sees the traffic of its members: who sends when, how often, how much, and who is reachable. It cannot read more than a member reads, forge or alter a frame, or hand someone removed anything new; it can withhold, which each member's second hub and the gossip turn limit (§ Hubs). Relays see a hub's beacon entry and lobby as a Pkarr key and an opaque value, under keys that change with every commit.
- Nothing on the network says that a member has frames waiting: they wait on the other members' devices until an edge opens (§ Catch-up). So checking a member's other profile for new messages from the same device ([04](04-profiles.md#checking-other-profiles)) does not cover mesh groups. Covering them would take a new sealed record written by the members who hold the frames (for example a flag on the edge's own Pkarr record, which only the two members of an edge can derive), and a publication on every send, which costs relay requests on the web; the community profile has its beacon's head instead.

## Open decisions

Private payments in a group (no note, or amounts hidden); more metadata (renaming the group, a description) in the same statement; a request to the group on reversible rails only, or split between members; approval of each entry before admission, and an expiry or use count on a link; multiple admins; a member key update (post-compromise security for a member's own key); files and media in groups, each behind a capability of its own; native transports on edges; a relay or GossipSub profile ([901](901-gossipsub.md)) for larger groups; groups past 32 on hubs (measured below, not allowed: without a hub they would fall back to a mesh nobody can afford); payments and payment notes between members that share no edge in a group on hubs (an edge opened for the payment, notes signed so hubs pass them on); a hub that keeps frames for members away longer than the logs reach; why a removal took two minutes to reach everyone at 128 members on hubs (harness only); a store for a member that never meets anyone holding what it missed (hold/1 per member, [4xx](4xx-store-and-forward.md)); typing passed on by hubs (signed, as reactions are, and rate limited by each hub), so that a group past 16 members sees everyone typing; a member's wake-up subscription shared with members it has no edge with in a group on hubs (a frame sealed to one member that hubs carry, § Wake-up push).
