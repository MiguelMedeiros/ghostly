- A command on a daemon that has stopped answering no longer waits for ever. A read or `daemon status` gives up after 15 seconds (`daemon status --timeout s` sets its own), a command that waits for the contact (`chat wait`, `file wait`, `--wait`) after its `--timeout` (or the default: 60, 300 and 30 s) plus 10 seconds, with code `timeout` (exit 4), `details.method` and the way out: `ghostly daemon restart`, which ends a daemon that does not answer `daemon stop` (SIGTERM, then SIGKILL) and starts a fresh one. A bot following the agent guide could otherwise hang at `chat wait` or `send` and never reach the next step. Every other command (`send`, `react`, `typing`, `invite create`, `chat rename`, `group create`, `settings set` and the like) gives up the same way when the daemon has not answered in 15 seconds, and is not sent then, so the daemon does not act on it later when it wakes. Once the daemon answers, a long-running send still takes as long as it needs, as do a subscription (`listen`) and `identity add`.
